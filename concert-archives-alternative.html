Before anything else

Concert Archives is genuinely good

A lot of "alternative" pages are written by people hoping you won't check. So: Concert Archives launched in 2013, runs on iOS, Android and the web, and by its own count holds well over a million documented concerts across hundreds of thousands of venues. It has a diary, photos and videos, notes, favourites, bucket lists, setlists on concert pages, friend follows, a newsfeed, group meetups and anniversary reminders for shows you went to years ago.

That is a more feature-complete product than ours. It's been built patiently by a community for over a decade and it has been covered in the sort of press most independent apps never get near. If you're already using it and happy, this page is not going to give you a reason to leave, and we're not going to invent one.

We make one of the alternatives here. On mobile coverage and sheer number of features, we lose. Those are the honest terms of the comparison.

The actual difference

One of us was actually there.

Every concert app in the world, Concert Archives included, runs on self-declared attendance. You say you were at a show, and the record accepts it. There's no malice in that — it's just how these products have always worked, because until phones had reliable location there wasn't another option.

Afterglow's one structural difference is that you can check in at the venue and have the show carry a verified stamp, visibly distinct from shows added afterwards. We're strict about it in the direction that costs us: shows you add retrospectively are not marked verified, because we weren't there to verify them. A stamp that's handed out freely isn't a stamp.

Whether that's worth anything depends entirely on what your list is for. If it's a private diary, you already know what you went to and verification adds nothing — go and use Concert Archives, it's better stocked. If the list is something you'd show people, the difference between a verified stamp and a ticked box is the whole difference.

What users report

Where people do get frustrated

Drawn from public app store reviews rather than our opinion — and worth reading alongside the fact that plenty of long-term users are perfectly happy.

One

App stability

The recurring complaint in reviews is bugginess — crashes, notifications that stick around, flows that don't quite behave. Reviewers who raise it usually say they're staying anyway because the underlying thing is worth the friction, which tells you something about how good the core product is.

Two

Festivals are fiddly

A specific and widely repeated one: adding a festival can pre-select every act on the bill, with no quick way to deselect, so you're manually unpicking a hundred entries to record the six sets you actually saw. If your history is festival-heavy, try that flow before committing an afternoon to it.

Three

You're helping build the database

Because it's community-powered, gaps exist — small venues, club nights, local bills — and filling them is part of the deal. Some people enjoy that and become prolific contributors. Others find it tedious. Neither reaction is wrong, but it's worth knowing which one you are before you start.

Four

Design is a matter of taste

Interface changes have drawn strong opinions in both directions, which is what happens to any product with a devoted long-term userbase. We're not going to pretend our design is objectively better — it's different, and newer, and built phone-first. Look at both and pick the one you'd want to open on the walk home.

Common questions

Concert Archives alternatives — FAQ

What's the best alternative to Concert Archives?

Honestly? For most people, nothing — it's already the most complete concert diary available. Afterglow is worth considering for one specific reason: GPS-verified check-ins, which no other concert app offers. If setlists, bucket lists and an iPhone app matter more to you than verification, stay where you are.

Does Concert Archives have a mobile app?

Yes — iOS and Android, alongside the website. Reviewers report stability problems and some awkward flows, particularly around festivals, but the apps exist and are maintained. On platform coverage they beat us: we have no iOS app.

Does Concert Archives verify attendance?

No — and nor does anything else except Afterglow. You add a show and the record accepts it. For a private diary that's completely fine. It only matters when the list is something other people see.

Can I move my history between them?

No. No concert app imports from another, there's no shared standard for attendance data, and ticket vendors don't expose order histories to third parties. Switching means re-typing. Our guide to finding every concert you've been to at least makes the rebuilding part systematic.

Can I use both?

Yes, though they overlap more than most pairings in this space. The workable split is Concert Archives for the deep back catalogue and Afterglow for checking in as you go. Both are free, so the only real cost is entering shows twice.
